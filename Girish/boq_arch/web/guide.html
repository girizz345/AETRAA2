How to measure on a drawing

Every length, area and count you take here is worked out in metres from the drawing's scale. It is listed with its figures and can go into the bill with its own formula. Nine steps, then a worked example.

1

Pick the drawing

Choose a sheet in the list on the left. Plans come first, then other sheets, then any document whose title block could not be read, such as a scan. A number beside a sheet means you have already measured that many things on it.

2

Check the scale - do this first

The box at the top right shows the sheet's scale. Every metre comes from it, so a wrong scale makes every measurement on that sheet wrong.

  • "Scale 1:152 at print · calibrated from the grid" - the platform checked the scale against the grid dimensions. It is ready to use.
  • "No scale yet" - lengths and areas can't be worked out until you set one. Counts and notes don't need a scale.

To set a scale: find a dimension you know on the drawing, such as a grid bay marked 6000. Press Set scale (K), click once on one end of it and once on the other, then type the real distance in millimetres (6000). If you trust the printed scale you can instead press Type 1:___ and enter, say, 100.

Check it: measure another dimension you know with the Length tool. If the readout agrees with the figure on the drawing, the scale is right. Sheets are often printed "to fit", so the scale written in the title block can be wrong.
3

Get close to what you are measuring

  • Scroll to zoom in and out. The point under the mouse stays where it is.
  • Drag with the mouse to move the drawing. This works in every tool, and dragging never places a point.
  • F or Fit shows the whole sheet again. The zoom level is shown in the toolbar (100% = whole sheet).

Zoom in far enough that you can click exactly on corners. Clicks snap to the ends of drawn lines: a pink square shows when you are on one. Turn Snap off in the toolbar if it gets in the way.

4

While you measure, a readout next to the mouse shows the figures as they grow:

  • Shift keeps the line exactly horizontal or vertical.
  • Ctrl+Z or Backspace takes back the last point. Esc abandons the measurement.
  • Bring the mouse near an edge of the drawing and it scrolls that way, so you can follow a long wall at high zoom.
5

Read the result

When you finish, the right-hand panel opens with a Measured box giving the figures in plain units:

You measuredYou get
a lengthLength in metres (L)
an area or rectangleArea in m² (A) and its Perimeter in metres (P) - the distance round the edge, used for skirtings and wall finishes
a countCount - the number of items (N)

After you save, the figure is also written on the drawing beside the shape (turn this off with Labels in the toolbar) and listed under On this sheet.

6

Say what it is (to put it in the bill)

A measurement on its own is only a figure. To turn it into bill quantities, fill in the panel:

  • Name - anything that helps you find it later ("Bedroom 2", "Corridor wall grid C").
  • Assembly - what it is, such as Block wall, Room finishes or Door. Each assembly turns the measurement into one or more bill lines using a formula. For example, a block wall gives blockwork = L × H and plaster to both faces = 2 × L × H. Leave it on None to keep the measurement without billing it.
  • Its settings - the boxes the assembly asks for, such as wall height (m) or thickness (mm).
  • Floor and Times (storeys) - on a "typical floor" plan that stands for several floors, put the number of floors in Times and every quantity is multiplied by it.
  • Deduction - tick it for a void or opening you want taken off, such as a shaft inside a floor area.

The table in the panel then shows each bill line, its formula and its quantity, for example Walls, 200mm thick - L*H - 60.20 m². Click Save.

To add or change assemblies and their formulas, open the Formulas tab.

7

Check your figures

  • On this sheet (right) lists everything measured on the sheet with its figures. Click one to open it again.
  • Manual take-off, all sheets adds up every bill line you have measured, across all sheets.
  • With Pan / select (V), click a shape on the drawing to open it, change it and save again.
8

Fix mistakes

  • Undo (Ctrl+Z) takes back the last point while measuring, or otherwise the last thing you saved, changed, deleted or erased. Hover over the button to see what it will undo.
  • Eraser (E): point at a shape (it turns red) and click to erase it. On a count, a click erases one mark and Shift+click the whole count.
9

Export to Excel

Export to Excel (right panel) produces:

  • the Take-off and Bill of Quantities again, with your measurements added to the right items. Each of your lines is marked MEASURED (MANUAL) and names the drawing, floor and formula;
  • a Manual Measurements workbook listing every measurement with its figures (L, A, P or N), times, bill line, formula and quantity;
  • each drawing you worked on as a marked-up PDF showing your shapes and notes.

The automatic take-off is not changed. The export sits beside it in a folder called with manual.

Worked example - a block wall on the 2nd floor

  1. Open 2ND FLOOR PLAN (A-07-005). The scale box says 1:152, calibrated from the grid, so the scale is fine.
  2. Press L for Length. Zoom in on the wall.
  3. Double-click at the wall's end, click at the corner, double-click at the other end. The readout shows Length 18.81 m.
  4. In the panel: Name Corridor wall; Assembly Block wall (both faces plastered); thickness 200; height 3.2; Times 1.
  5. The table shows Walls, 200mm thick: L*H = 18.81 × 3.2 = 60.20 m² and plaster to walls: 2*L*H = 120.40 m². Click Save.
  6. The drawing now reads Corridor wall · 18.81 m beside the wall. The totals at the bottom right include the two new lines.
  7. Export to Excel. Both lines appear in the bill under Masonry and Finishes, marked MEASURED (MANUAL).
